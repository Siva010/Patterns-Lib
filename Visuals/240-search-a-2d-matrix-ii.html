⚠ Why Act 1 is not merely slower — it is wrong. Binary search does not need "sorted rows". It needs a total order on the sequence it indexes: for every i < j, a[i] ≤ a[j]. Only then does "a[mid] > target" license "discard everything at index ≥ mid". LC 74 hands you that order for free. LC 240 does not: at every row boundary the tape steps backwards (marked | on the strip), so a comparison at mid says nothing about the cells beyond it. The code still terminates, still runs in O(log mn), and still prints a confident answer. It is just not the right one.

1 / 3LC 74 — flatten and binary-search, because the matrix really is one sorted array.

2 / 3LC 240 (here) — you cannot flatten, so walk the staircase. Standing at the top-right corner, the cell you see is the largest in its row and the smallest in its column. Too big → its whole column is too big, step left. Too small → its whole row is too small, step down. Each step retires a full row or column, so at most m + n steps — watch the live area drop in the stat line.

3 / 3LC 378 — neither machine. Binary-search the answer, and use this staircase as the counting routine inside it.

Act 2: O(m + n) time · O(1) space  ·  Act 1: O(log mn) and wrong — sorted rows and sorted columns do not add up to a total order.
